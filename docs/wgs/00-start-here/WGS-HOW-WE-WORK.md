# How WGS Works

This is the scalable studio pipeline. Small projects may combine stages; complex
projects should record each stage in the project folder.

## 1. Discovery / Story

Understand the business, audience, market, competitors, offer, objectives,
desired feeling, evidence, constraints, and what is already approved. Separate
observed facts from assumptions and questions.

## 2. Creative Brief

Define the problem, audience, objective, message, primary action, scope,
constraints, success evidence, and non-goals. The brief is a decision tool, not a
decorative summary.

## 3. Creative Territories

Develop three genuinely different worlds. Each territory defines its concept,
emotional direction, references, typography, colour, composition, imagery,
visual motif, motion, interaction, implementation implications, and risks.

Do not create the same layout three times in different colours.

## 4. Previs

Translate the idea into a sitemap, wireframe, interaction map, scroll sequence,
responsive transformation, and rough motion storyboard. Resolve structural and
technical contradictions before polishing frames.

## 5. Look Development

Develop typography, materials, photography, texture, colour, geometry, 3D,
interface detail, and visual polish. Test the visual language on real content,
desktop, and mobile—not only one hero frame.

## 6. Production

Produce in Figma and/or implementation using the approved stack and the smallest
appropriate toolset. Preserve accessibility, content truth, and maintainability.

## 7. Dailies

Review what works, what feels generic, what is unclear, what is difficult to
implement, what should be removed, and what needs another test. Record decisions
in `docs/wgs/projects/_templates/DAILIES.md` or the project equivalent.

## 8. Finaling

Validate desktop, mobile, motion, reduced motion, accessibility, forms, booking,
analytics, SEO, performance, browser behaviour, and final content. Record known
limitations and real results separately from planned outcomes.
